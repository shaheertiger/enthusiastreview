Types of Power Supplies for High-TDP GPUs

Not every 750W PSU is built the same way, and with a card pulling 215-250W at the die, the differences actually matter. Here's what to know before you pick one.

Wattage Sizing for High-TDP GPUs

NVIDIA's official recommendation for the RTX 2080 is a 650W PSU minimum, and for the RTX 2080 Ti it's 650-700W depending on the card partner. In practice, we recommend sizing higher than the minimum: a 650W unit is fine for a 2080 paired with a mid-range CPU, but a 2080 Ti paired with a high-core-count CPU like a Ryzen 9 3900X or 3950X pushes combined system draw close enough to 650W that you lose all headroom for spikes, overclocking, or future upgrades. 750W is the sweet spot for most 2080/2080 Ti builds, and 850W gives you real breathing room if you're running an overclocked Ti alongside a 12+ core CPU.

Single vs. Dual 8-Pin PCIe Requirements

Reference-design RTX 2080 cards typically need a single 8-pin PCIe connector, while the RTX 2080 Ti — especially Founders Edition and factory-overclocked AIB cards — almost always requires two 8-pin PCIe connectors. Always check your specific card's connector requirement before buying a PSU, and make sure the PSU has at least that many native PCIe cables (not just spare 6-pin-to-8-pin adapters, which are a common cause of intermittent power issues under load). Every PSU on this list has at least two 8-pin PCIe connectors, and the 750W-850W units have four, which also leaves room for a second GPU or a high-draw peripheral down the line.

Transient Spike Headroom

This is the piece budget PSUs get wrong most often. Modern high-TDP GPUs — including the RTX 2080 Ti — don't draw power in a flat line; they spike well above their rated TDP for microseconds at a time during load transitions. A 2080 Ti rated at 250W TDP can transient-spike to 350W+ for a few milliseconds. A well-built PSU with tight voltage regulation and enough capacitor headroom absorbs these spikes without tripping its over-power protection (OPP) or sagging voltage. A cheap, loosely-regulated unit running near its rated capacity can shut down or destabilize the system on a spike it should never have noticed. This is the single biggest reason we favor 80 PLUS Gold-or-better units with a strong single 12V rail over the cheapest wattage-matched PSU you can find.

80 PLUS Efficiency Certification

80 PLUS Gold is the practical floor we recommend for a 2080/2080 Ti build — it guarantees at least 87-90% efficiency at typical loads, which means less wasted heat, quieter fan curves, and lower electricity draw than a Bronze unit at the same wattage. Platinum and Titanium units exist and squeeze out another few percentage points of efficiency, but the price premium rarely pays for itself for a single-GPU gaming build; Gold is where the value curve flattens out.

PSU Buying Guide for RTX 2080 / 2080 Ti Builds

Match Wattage to Your Full System, Not Just the GPU

The GPU's TDP is the biggest single number, but it's not the only draw on the rail. Add up your CPU's TDP, 2-4 storage drives, RAM, fans, and any AIO pump, then compare the total against the PSU's rated wattage with at least 20-30% headroom remaining. That headroom isn't waste — it's what keeps the PSU running in its efficient middle load range and gives it room to absorb transient spikes without stress.

Count Your PCIe Connectors Before You Buy

Check your specific GPU's power connector requirement (one 8-pin vs. two 8-pin) before ordering a PSU, and make sure the unit has that many native connectors — not 6-pin cables you'd need to adapter up. Adapters are a common, avoidable point of failure under sustained load.

Choose Modular or Semi-Modular for Airflow

Fully modular PSUs let you leave out any cable you don't need, which reduces clutter and improves case airflow — a real benefit when a 2080 Ti is already dumping 250W+ of heat into the case. Semi-modular units save money but leave the 24-pin and CPU cables permanently attached, which is fine in larger cases but worth avoiding in tight, airflow-limited builds.

Don't Skip the 80 PLUS Rating

Efficiency certification isn't just an environmental checkbox — a higher-efficiency PSU wastes less power as heat, which means a cooler-running, quieter, longer-lived unit. 80 PLUS Gold is our floor recommendation for any 2080/2080 Ti build; anything lower and you're leaving voltage regulation quality on the table along with the efficiency.

Check Physical Clearance Before You Order

Most 750W-850W ATX PSUs run 140-160mm deep. Compact and mid-tower cases with front-mounted radiator space or cable routing channels can be tighter than they look — check your case's PSU clearance spec against the unit's listed depth before ordering, especially if you're also running a front AIO radiator.

Calculating PSU Wattage for an RTX 2080 Ti + High-End CPU

Here's the simplest way to size a PSU correctly for a 2080 Ti paired with a demanding CPU:

  1. Start with GPU TDP: RTX 2080 Ti = 250W (use the manufacturer's listed TDP for AIB/overclocked cards, which can run 260-280W).
  2. Add CPU TDP: A high-end CPU like a Ryzen 9 3900X (105W) or 3950X (105W) under an all-core load can pull close to or above its rated TDP.
  3. Add the rest of the system: budget roughly 50-80W combined for motherboard, RAM, 2-3 storage drives, and case fans.
  4. Total it up: 250W (GPU) + 105W (CPU) + ~65W (rest of system) = ~420W of realistic continuous draw.
  5. Add 30% headroom for transient spikes and efficiency: 420W x 1.3 ≈ 550W minimum — which is why we recommend sizing to 750W rather than the bare minimum, since it leaves real margin for overclocking, aging capacitors, and future upgrades rather than running the PSU at its rated ceiling every session.

Quick tip: If you're overclocking either the GPU or CPU, or running an AIB Ti card with a factory power limit above 250W, size up to 850W rather than 750W — overclocking increases both sustained draw and the size of transient spikes, and running a PSU consistently near its rated capacity shortens its usable lifespan.

Cable Management and Connector Checklist

  • Confirm your GPU needs one or two 8-pin PCIe connectors and that the PSU has that many native cables
  • Use one 8-pin cable per connector where possible rather than splitting a single cable into two connectors, even if the cable supports it
  • Route the 24-pin ATX and CPU (4+4 or 8-pin) cables behind the motherboard tray before connecting peripherals
  • Leave unused modular cables unplugged and out of the case entirely — don't coil them behind the PSU shroud where they block airflow
  • Double-check the CPU power connector count on your motherboard (some boards need both an 8-pin and a supplemental 4-pin)

Is ATX 3.0 Relevant for a 2080/2080 Ti Build?

ATX 3.0 PSUs are built around the 12VHPWR connector and tighter transient response specs designed for newer GPUs. The RTX 2080 and 2080 Ti use standard 8-pin PCIe connectors, not 12VHPWR, so ATX 3.0 isn't required for these cards. That said, an ATX 3.0-rated unit like the EVGA SuperNOVA GA series still delivers better real-world transient response than an older non-ATX-3.0 design at the same price, and it future-proofs your PSU for a next-generation GPU upgrade that does use the newer connector — worth considering if you plan to keep the PSU through your next GPU refresh.

FAQ

What wattage PSU do I need for an RTX 2080 Ti?

NVIDIA's official minimum is 650W, but we recommend 750W for most builds and 850W if you're pairing the Ti with a high-core-count CPU like a Ryzen 9 3900X or 3950X, or if you're overclocking either component. The extra headroom absorbs transient spikes and keeps the PSU running in its efficient load range rather than near its rated ceiling.

Does the RTX 2080 (non-Ti) need less power than the 2080 Ti?

Yes. The standard RTX 2080 has a 215W TDP versus the 2080 Ti's 250W, and most reference 2080 cards use a single 8-pin PCIe connector rather than the Ti's typical two. A 650W PSU is generally sufficient for a 2080 build with a mid-range CPU.

How many 8-pin PCIe connectors does an RTX 2080 Ti need?

Most RTX 2080 Ti cards, including the Founders Edition, need two 8-pin PCIe connectors. Always check your specific card's spec sheet, since some reference-clocked partner cards use a single 8-pin plus a 6-pin configuration instead.

What is a transient power spike, and why does it matter for the 2080 Ti?

A transient spike is a very brief (millisecond-scale) surge in power draw well above a GPU's rated TDP, caused by rapid load changes. The RTX 2080 Ti is known for spiking meaningfully above its 250W TDP during these moments. A PSU with tight voltage regulation and adequate headroom absorbs these spikes silently; an undersized or loosely regulated unit can trip its protection circuits and shut the system down.

Is 80 PLUS Gold good enough, or should I get Platinum/Titanium?

80 PLUS Gold is our recommended floor for a 2080/2080 Ti build and delivers excellent efficiency and voltage regulation at a reasonable price. Platinum and Titanium units offer marginally better efficiency but at a real price premium that mostly matters for 24/7 workstation or server use rather than a gaming build.

Do I need a fully modular PSU for a 2080 Ti build?

It's not strictly required, but it's recommended. Fully modular cabling lets you skip unused cables entirely, improving airflow around a GPU that's already generating significant heat under load. Semi-modular units are a reasonable budget compromise; fully non-modular units make cable management noticeably harder in smaller cases.

Can I reuse an older PSU for a new RTX 2080 Ti build?

Only if it meets the wattage, connector, and age criteria above. A PSU older than 5-7 years has typically degraded capacitors that reduce its effective output and transient response even if its rated wattage looks sufficient on paper — we'd recommend replacing anything older than that generation rather than risking it under a 250W-TDP card.

Will an underpowered PSU damage my RTX 2080 Ti?

An underpowered or failing PSU is more likely to cause instability, random shutdowns, or voltage sag under load than direct GPU damage, since most GPUs and PSUs include protection circuits. That said, a poorly regulated unit can still cause real problems over time, so it's not a risk worth taking with a 250W-TDP card.
